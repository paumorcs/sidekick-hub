Notion
Documentation · Wikis · Databases · Project Tracking
What It Is

Notion is a flexible workspace tool that combines docs, databases, task lists, and wikis into one place. Imagine a notebook, a spreadsheet, and a project board fused together — that's Notion. You can write a page, embed a checklist, add a table, and link everything together without ever leaving the app.

Why We Use It

Many of our clients run their entire knowledge base inside Notion — SOPs, project briefs, content calendars, client info. As a Tech Sidekick, you'll create and maintain these pages so the client's information stays organized, searchable, and easy for their team to use. A clean Notion workspace saves hours of confusion every week.

Step-by-Step Guide · Zero to Hero
  1. 1
    Sign in at notion.so. Your workspaces appear on the left sidebar — these are like separate folders for different clients or projects.
  2. 2
    To create a new page, click + New Page at the bottom of the sidebar. Give it a title at the top, then press Enter to start writing.
  3. 3
    Type / on a blank line to open the Slash Command Menu — this is your superpower. From here you can insert headings, tables, checklists, callouts, dividers, and dozens more blocks.
  4. 4
    To create a database, type /table and press Enter. Add columns by clicking + Add a property. Each column can be text, dates, dropdowns, checkboxes, and more.
  5. 5
    To organize pages, drag and drop them in the sidebar. You can nest pages inside other pages — perfect for grouping related SOPs under one parent.
  6. 6
    To share a page, click Share in the top-right. Add the client's email or toggle "Share to web" — but always confirm permission level (Can edit, Can comment, Can view).

GoHighLevel (GHL)
CRM · Pipelines · Conversations · Automations
What It Is

GoHighLevel — usually called GHL — is an all-in-one CRM and marketing platform built for agencies and service businesses. It handles client contacts, sales pipelines, follow-up automations, email and SMS campaigns, calendars, and a unified inbox for all client communication. Think of it as a control center for the client's entire customer journey.

Why We Use It

Many of our clients run their entire sales and marketing operation through GHL. As a Tech Sidekick, you'll keep contacts updated, move leads through the pipeline, respond to messages from the unified inbox, and make sure no automation breaks. A well-maintained GHL means leads don't fall through the cracks — and that translates directly to client revenue.

Step-by-Step · Navigating the Dashboard
  1. 1
    Log in at the client's GHL subdomain (e.g. app.gohighlevel.com or a custom branded URL). Always use your own staff login — never share credentials.
  2. 2
    The Left Sidebar is your main navigation. Key sections: Dashboard (overview), Conversations (unified inbox), Contacts (the CRM), Opportunities (pipelines), Calendars, and Automation.
  3. 3
    Click Conversations daily. This shows all incoming messages — email, SMS, Facebook, Instagram, webchat — in one inbox. Reply directly from inside GHL.
  4. 4
    Click Contacts to view all leads and customers. Use the search bar at the top to find a specific person fast. Each contact has tabs for activity, tasks, notes, and message history.
  5. 5
    Click Opportunities to see the visual pipeline board. Each card represents a lead, and stages represent steps in the sales process.
🎯 Workflow · Changing a Lead's Pipeline Stage
  1. 1
    From the sidebar, click Opportunities. The board view appears, showing all pipeline stages as columns (e.g. New Lead → Contacted → Qualified → Proposal Sent → Won/Lost).
  2. 2
    Find the lead's card in their current stage column. You can use the filter bar at the top to narrow down by source, owner, or status.
  3. 3
    Drag and drop the lead card from their current stage column into the new stage column. The card animates into place and the change is saved instantly.
  4. 4
    Click on the card to open the lead's details. Add a note explaining why the stage changed (e.g. "Sent proposal Sept 5 — awaiting feedback").
  5. 5
    If the move triggers an automation (like an email sequence), check that under Automation → Workflows. Always verify automated emails have the correct content before relying on them.

Loom
Screen + Camera Recording · Async Updates · Walkthroughs
What It Is

Loom lets you record your screen — and optionally your face — and instantly turn it into a shareable video link. No file downloads, no email attachments. You record, click stop, copy the link, and paste it anywhere. It's the fastest way to explain something visually.

Why We Use It

Loom replaces long emails. Instead of writing three paragraphs explaining a problem, you record a 90-second video showing exactly what you see. Clients understand in seconds what would take ten minutes to type. Use Loom for: client updates, walkthroughs, bug reports, training new teammates, and anything where "show, don't tell" is faster.

Step-by-Step · Setting Up Screen + Camera Recording
  1. 1
    Install the Loom Chrome Extension. Search "Loom" in the Chrome Web Store, click Add to Chrome, then pin the icon to your toolbar so it's always one click away.
  2. 2
    Click the Loom icon in your toolbar. A small panel appears with recording mode options.
  3. 3
    Choose your mode: Screen + Camera (recommended for client updates — your face appears as a small bubble), Screen Only (for tutorials and demos), or Camera Only (for personal video messages).
  4. 4
    Choose your recording area: Full Desktop (entire screen), Current Tab (just one Chrome tab), or Custom Size (a draggable rectangle).
  5. 5
    Confirm your Microphone and Camera are selected correctly. Click the dropdowns to switch devices if needed.
  6. 6
    Click the red Start Recording button. A 3-second countdown begins, then recording starts.
Step-by-Step · Pausing, Stopping, Sharing
  1. 1
    While recording, the Loom mini-toolbar appears on your screen with controls: Pause, Stop, Cancel, and Webcam toggle.
  2. 2
    Click Pause if you need a break — recording resumes from where you left off when you click Pause again.
  3. 3
    Click Stop when finished. Loom automatically uploads and opens the recording in a new browser tab.
  4. 4
    Add a clear title at the top of the video page. Good titles describe the topic: "Walkthrough — September Reporting Errors" rather than "Untitled Recording."
  5. 5
    Click Copy Link at the top right. Paste this directly into Slack, email, or WhatsApp. The recipient can watch instantly — no Loom account required.
Step-by-Step · Trimming Video Ends
  1. 1
    Open your video on Loom's website. Below the player, click Edit → Trim Video.
  2. 2
    Two markers appear on the timeline — a left handle and a right handle. Drag the left handle rightward to cut off the awkward "fumbling to start" intro.
  3. 3
    Drag the right handle leftward to cut off any "uhh, that's it" outro at the end.
  4. 4
    Click Save. The trimmed video saves to your library and the share link automatically updates — anyone who already has the link will now see the trimmed version.
💡 Pro Tip · Look Professional
Before hitting record: close personal tabs, mute your phone, hide your bookmarks bar (Ctrl + Shift + B), and check that nothing sensitive is visible on your desktop. A clean, professional screen builds instant trust with clients. Keep recordings under 3 minutes whenever possible — concise wins.

Canva
Brand Templates · Social Posts · Presentations · Exports
What It Is

Canva is a browser-based design tool that makes professional graphics easy — even with zero design background. Thousands of pre-built templates, drag-and-drop editing, and brand kits let you create on-brand visuals in minutes instead of hours.

Why We Use It

Clients need consistent, on-brand visuals fast — Instagram posts, LinkedIn graphics, presentation slides, thumbnails, ad creatives. Canva lets you produce all of that quickly using the client's brand colors, fonts, and logo, without needing to know Photoshop or Illustrator.

Step-by-Step · Brand Templates & Drag-and-Drop
  1. 1
    Sign in at canva.com using your team's account. From the dashboard, your client's Brand Templates appear in the sidebar — start there for any branded content.
  2. 2
    If creating from scratch, click Create a design in the top right. Search the format you need: "Instagram Post," "LinkedIn Banner," "Facebook Cover," etc.
  3. 3
    Use the Brand Kit tab on the left sidebar to access the client's logo, colors, and fonts. These should always be the foundation of any design.
  4. 4
    To add text: click Text in the left sidebar, then click any preset style. To edit existing text, just click directly on it on the canvas.
  5. 5
    To add images: drag and drop them from the Uploads tab or from your desktop directly onto the canvas. To replace an image in a template frame, drag the new image directly on top of the existing one.
  6. 6
    To duplicate a page (great for multi-slide carousels), click the page, then click the Duplicate Page icon (two-squares icon) at the top of the page.
Step-by-Step · Exporting for Instagram & Facebook
  1. 1
    Click Share in the top right of the editor.
  2. 2
    Click Download.
  3. 3
    Choose the file type: PNG for social posts (best image quality), JPG for smaller file sizes, MP4 for animated/video designs, PDF for documents and presentations.
  4. 4
    For Instagram, ensure dimensions are 1080×1080 px (feed) or 1080×1920 px (Stories/Reels). Canva auto-sizes these if you started from the right template.
  5. 5
    Click Download — the file saves to your computer's default downloads folder. Always rename the file with the date and platform (e.g. IG_Sept5_LaunchPost.png) before sending to a client or scheduler.

Figma
UI Design · Mockups · Asset Export · Design Systems
What It Is

Figma is the design tool used by professional designers worldwide. It runs in the browser, allows real-time collaboration, and is where most modern websites, apps, and brand systems are designed. As a Sidekick, you'll often be asked to view client design files, extract assets (logos, icons, images) for social use, and make minor text edits.

Why We Use It

Designers and agencies hand off branded assets, mockups, and content visuals through Figma. Knowing how to navigate a Figma file, find layers, and export images means you can pull what you need without bothering a designer for every small request — making you instantly more valuable to creative-driven clients.

Step-by-Step · Viewing Design Files
  1. 1
    Sign in at figma.com. Open the file shared by the client or your team lead — files appear in your Recents or shared Team folder.
  2. 2
    You'll land on the canvas. Use Ctrl + Scroll to zoom in/out, and hold Spacebar + drag to pan around.
  3. 3
    The Layers panel on the left shows the file structure. Click any layer name to instantly select that element on the canvas.
  4. 4
    Press Shift + 1 to fit the entire design in your view. Press Shift + 2 to zoom into your selected element.
Step-by-Step · Exporting Assets for Social Media
  1. 1
    Press V to switch to the Move tool. Click the asset you want to export — image, icon, logo, or a whole frame.
  2. 2
    Look at the right panel and scroll down to the Export section.
  3. 3
    Click the + next to Export to add an export setting. Choose: 1x PNG (standard), 2x PNG (high resolution for social), or SVG (logos and icons that need to scale).
  4. 4
    Click Export [layer name]. The file saves to your downloads folder.
  5. 5
    To export multiple assets at once, hold Shift and click each layer in the Layers panel, then click Export — Figma will save them all into one ZIP.
Step-by-Step · Editing Text
  1. 1
    Press T to switch to the Text tool, OR double-click any existing text on the canvas to start editing.
  2. 2
    Make your change. Press Esc when done.
  3. 3
    If the text doesn't look right after editing, check the right panel for font size, line height, and weight. Don't change the font family unless explicitly asked — designers chose it intentionally.

Meta Ads Manager
Facebook & Instagram Paid Advertising
What It Is

Meta Ads Manager is the platform for running paid ads on Facebook and Instagram. It's where campaigns are built, audiences are defined, creatives (images/videos) are uploaded, budgets are set, and performance is tracked — all in one dashboard.

Why We Use It

Paid social ads are one of the fastest ways clients reach new customers. As a Socials Sidekick, you'll support campaign setup, monitor performance, upload creative, organize reports, and flag what's working vs what's underperforming. Even basic tasks here move real revenue for clients.

🏗️ The Three-Level Structure (Most Important Concept)

Every Meta ad campaign is built using a strict three-level structure. Understanding this hierarchy is the single most important thing about using Ads Manager. Each level controls a different decision:

📌 LEVEL 1 · CAMPAIGN
Sets the Objective. What does the client want this ad to achieve? Sales? Leads? Traffic? Engagement? One campaign sets one goal.
↓
🎯 LEVEL 2 · AD SET
Sets the Targeting. Who sees the ad? Where (Facebook/Instagram/placements)? How much (daily budget)? When (schedule)? One campaign can have multiple ad sets — one per audience.
↓
🖼️ LEVEL 3 · AD
Sets the Creative. The actual image, video, headline, caption, and call-to-action button (e.g. "Shop Now") that people see. One ad set can run multiple ad creatives at the same time.
Step-by-Step · Navigating Ads Manager
  1. 1
    Go to business.facebook.com. Always operate inside Business Manager — never use a personal Facebook account for client work.
  2. 2
    From the top-left dropdown, switch to the client's Ad Account. Confirm the correct one — running a campaign on the wrong account is a costly mistake.
  3. 3
    Click Ads Manager in the main menu. The home view shows three tabs at the top: Campaigns, Ad Sets, and Ads — these match the three-level structure above.
  4. 4
    Use the date range filter at the top right to set the time period you want to view (last 7 days, last 30 days, custom).
  5. 5
    To monitor performance, look at the columns: Reach (how many people saw the ad), Impressions (total times shown), CTR (click-through rate — aim for above 1%), Cost per Result (most important — what you're paying per outcome), and Frequency (above 3 means audience fatigue is starting).
  6. 6
    If something looks off, never delete an ad. Toggle it Off using the on/off switch on the left side, then escalate to your team lead.
📊 Key Metrics to Watch
MetricWhat it meansHealthy range
CTR (Click-Through Rate)% of people who clicked the ad after seeing itAbove 1% (above 2% is excellent)
CPM (Cost per 1,000 impressions)Cost to show ad to 1,000 peopleVaries by industry — flag spikes > 30%
CPC (Cost per Click)Average price for one clickLower is better — context-dependent
Cost per ResultWhat you pay per goal (lead, sale, etc.)Compare to client's target — flag if 2x higher
FrequencyAvg times same person saw the ad1.5–3.0 — above 3 means refresh creative
ROAS (Return on Ad Spend)$ revenue per $1 spent on ads2x or higher = profitable; below 1 = losing money
💡 Pro Tip · The Sandwich
Remember Meta's three levels like a sandwich: Campaign = the bread (the objective). Ad Set = the filling (who and how much). Ad = the look (what people see). Once that structure clicks in your head, the whole platform makes sense.

Amazon Seller Central
Inventory · Listings · Orders · Account Health
What It Is

Amazon Seller Central is the dashboard Amazon sellers use to manage their entire business on Amazon — product listings, inventory, orders, customer messages, advertising, and account health. If a client sells on Amazon, this is mission control.

Why We Use It

Amazon clients depend on this platform for their revenue. A stockout, listing error, or unanswered policy violation can cost hundreds of dollars per day. Your job: keep inventory healthy, listings accurate, and account health green so the client can focus on growth.

Step-by-Step · Checking Inventory Levels
  1. 1
    Log in at sellercentral.amazon.com using your client's credentials or your assigned staff access.
  2. 2
    From the top menu, hover over Inventory → click Manage All Inventory. This shows every active product and its current stock levels.
  3. 3
    Look at the Available column — that's how many units are ready to sell right now. Items below 30 units of stock get flagged to the team lead immediately.
  4. 4
    Look at the Days of Supply column — Amazon's estimate of how many days the current stock will last based on recent sales velocity. Below 30 days is a warning, below 14 days is critical.
  5. 5
    Check Inventory → Fix Stranded Inventory. These are items Amazon is holding but can't sell — they sit there racking up storage fees. Always clear these.
Step-by-Step · Finding the SKU

SKU = Stock Keeping Unit, the seller's internal product code. ASIN = Amazon's product code (10 characters, starts with B0). You'll need both regularly.

  1. 1
    Go to Inventory → Manage All Inventory.
  2. 2
    The SKU column appears in the table — usually the second or third column from the left. If it's hidden, click Preferences at the top right and toggle it on.
  3. 3
    To search by SKU directly, paste it into the search bar at the top of the inventory page and press Enter.
  4. 4
    To copy a SKU quickly, click directly on the SKU text in the table — most browsers select it for one-click copying.
Step-by-Step · Managing Product Listings
  1. 1
    From the inventory list, find the product and click Edit (right side of the row).
  2. 2
    The listing page has tabs: Vital Info (title, brand), Offer (price, condition), Images, Description, Keywords, More Details. Update only the tabs you've been instructed to update.
  3. 3
    Title best practices: under 200 characters, includes brand, product type, and 2–3 key features. Never use ALL CAPS or special characters.
  4. 4
    Bullet points: 5 max, each under 200 characters, lead with the benefit not the feature.
  5. 5
    Click Save and Finish at the bottom. Changes go live within 15 minutes to a few hours.

⚠️ Critical Rule
Never delete a product — use Close Listing instead. Deleting permanently removes review history and ranking. Closing keeps everything safe and the listing can be reactivated anytime.

Shopify
Online Store · Products · Orders · Customers
What It Is

Shopify is the platform clients use to run their own online store. It handles product listings, inventory, checkout, payments, and order fulfillment — all from one admin dashboard. Think of it as the storefront and back office combined.

Why We Use It

Clients depend on a smooth-running Shopify store for daily revenue. As a Sourcing Sidekick, you'll process orders, look up customer details, update product info, and keep inventory accurate. Quick, error-free order handling means happy customers and repeat sales.

Step-by-Step · Processing Orders
  1. 1
    Log in at yourstore.myshopify.com/admin using your assigned staff account.
  2. 2
    Click Orders in the left sidebar. The default view shows all orders — sortable by date, status, or financial state.
  3. 3
    Filter by Unfulfilled to focus on orders that need processing.
  4. 4
    Click any order to see customer details, shipping address, items ordered, and payment status.
📦 The Order Fulfillment Flow

Every order moves through these exact steps. Follow the flow strictly — skipping a step causes errors that ripple to customers, inventory, and accounting.

1
Order Received. Customer places order. Status: Paid + Unfulfilled. The order appears in Orders → Unfulfilled.
↓
2
Verify Payment. Confirm Financial Status shows "Paid" (not Pending or Refunded). Never fulfill an unpaid order.
↓
3
Verify Stock. Click each item — confirm there's enough inventory. If short, contact warehouse/supplier before fulfilling.
↓
4
Print Packing Slip. Click More actions → Print packing slip. Send to warehouse or print yourself.
↓
5
Pick & Pack. Pull items, package securely, attach packing slip and shipping label.
↓
6
Add Tracking + Mark Fulfilled. In the order, scroll to Fulfillment → click Fulfill items → enter tracking number → click Fulfill. Customer gets automated email.
↓
7
Confirm Shipped. Order status now reads "Fulfilled." Mark complete on internal task tracker.
Step-by-Step · Looking Up Customer Details
  1. 1
    From the sidebar, click Customers.
  2. 2
    Use the search bar at the top. You can search by name, email, or phone number.
  3. 3
    Click the customer's name to open their profile. You'll see total orders, total spent, default address, accepted marketing status, and order history.
  4. 4
    To see all of a customer's past orders, scroll down to the Orders section in their profile — every order is listed.
Step-by-Step · Updating Inventory
  1. 1
    From the sidebar, click Products → Inventory.
  2. 2
    The page shows every variant with its current stock count.
  3. 3
    To update one item, click the quantity field next to the variant and type the new number. Click Save.
  4. 4
    For bulk updates, click Update Quantities at the top — you can adjust multiple items at once and add a reason note (e.g. "Stocktake — Sept 5").
⚠️ Critical Rule
Never delete a product — use Archive (Actions → Archive). Deleting wipes order history tied to that product. Archiving hides it from the storefront while preserving everything in reports.

Keepa
Amazon Price History · Buy Box Tracking · Sales Rank
What It Is

Keepa is a free Chrome extension that adds a price history graph directly to every Amazon product page. It tracks how the price has changed over time, who currently has the Buy Box, how many sellers compete on the listing, and how the product's sales rank has moved. It's pure data — beautifully visualized.

Why We Use It

Amazon prices change constantly. Before sourcing any product, you need to know: Is the price stable or crashing? Is Amazon itself competing on this listing? Is the product actually selling? Keepa answers all of these in one glance — meaning clients price confidently, source safely, and avoid losing money on products in price-crash territory.

Step-by-Step · Reading the Price History Graph
  1. 1
    Install the Keepa Chrome Extension from the Chrome Web Store. Free for basic use. Once installed, the graph auto-appears on every Amazon product page.
  2. 2
    Go to any Amazon product page and scroll down past the buy box. The Keepa graph appears — colorful lines plotted over a timeline showing 30, 90, or 365 days of history.
  3. 3
    Use the time-range buttons at the top of the graph to switch between Day · Week · Month · 3 Months · Year · All. Default to 90 days for sourcing decisions — long enough to spot trends, short enough to be relevant.
  4. 4
    Use the checkboxes below the graph to toggle which lines show. Start with just Amazon, New (3rd party), and Buy Box visible — the others are noise for most sourcing decisions.
🎨 The Keepa Color Code · What Every Line Means

Each colored line tells a different story. Memorize what these mean — it's the foundation of every Keepa-based sourcing decision.

🩷 Pink — Buy Box Price
The most important line. Shows what price wins the "Add to Cart" button at any moment. Stable or rising = healthy product. Sharply dropping = market is crashing.
🟠 Orange — Amazon Price
When Amazon itself is selling the product. If this line is consistently visible, Amazon is your competitor — extremely difficult to win against. Avoid sourcing.
🔵 Blue — New (3rd-Party) Price
Shows the lowest price from third-party sellers. Useful for understanding marketplace competition outside of Amazon.
🟢 Green — Sales Rank
Lower BSR = sells more. Look for frequent downward dips — each dip is roughly a sale. Many dips = active product. Flat line = sitting unsold.
🟣 Purple — Used Price
Track for used-condition listings. Rarely relevant for new product sourcing, but important if client deals in pre-owned goods.
⚫ Gray — New Offer Count
Shows how many sellers are on the listing. A sudden spike means new competition entered. A drop means sellers exited (could be opportunity or warning).

Google Workspace · Sheets
Spreadsheets · Financial Trackers · Data Entry · Sharing
What It Is

Google Sheets is a cloud-based spreadsheet tool that lives in your browser. It's like Excel, but updates in real time when multiple people work on it together. For Accounts Sidekicks, it's where most expense logs, invoice trackers, and financial reports get built and maintained.

Why We Use It

Many clients track their finances in Sheets because it's free, accessible, and shareable. Your job is to keep those sheets clean, accurate, and up to date — so the client always knows where their money stands. Clean sheets = clean books = trustworthy decisions.

Step-by-Step · Basic Data Entry & Clean Formatting
  1. 1
    Open Google Sheets via sheets.google.com or click the grid icon in Gmail and select Sheets.
  2. 2
    Click on a cell to select it. Type to enter data. Press Tab to move right, Enter to move down, Shift + Tab to move left.
  3. 3
    Format your headers properly: Select row 1, then go to Format → Bold, set background color, and freeze the row via View → Freeze → 1 row.
  4. 4
    Format numbers as currency: select the column → click Format → Number → Currency. Or click the $ icon in the toolbar.
  5. 5
    Format dates consistently: select the column → Format → Number → Date. Use one date format across the whole sheet.
  6. 6
    Apply alternating row colors: select the data range → Format → Alternating colors. Makes long lists infinitely easier to read.
Step-by-Step · Sharing Settings
  1. 1
    Click the green Share button in the top right.
  2. 2
    Type the recipient's email. Always set the correct permission: Viewer (read-only), Commenter (can comment but not edit), Editor (full access).
  3. 3
    For client sheets containing financial data, default to Commenter for clients — they can flag issues without accidentally breaking formulas.
  4. 4
    Never enable "Anyone with the link" for sheets with sensitive financial data. Always use specific email addresses.
  5. 5
    Add a note in the share message confirming what the recipient can do (e.g. "View-only access — please reply with any flags").

Microsoft Office · Excel
Spreadsheets · Pivot Tables · Financial Reporting · Macros
What It Is

Microsoft Excel is the industry standard for spreadsheet work. It's more powerful than Google Sheets for large datasets, complex formulas, and advanced features like pivot tables, macros, and conditional formatting. Many accounting firms, financial advisors, and corporate clients work exclusively in Excel.

Why We Use It

When clients send you .xlsx files, you'll be expected to open them, understand them, and update them — often with no Sheets equivalent. Mastering Excel basics (and especially the keyboard shortcuts) makes you faster, more accurate, and infinitely more valuable on financial tasks.

Step-by-Step · Basic Formulas & Formatting
  1. 1
    Open Excel and create or open a file. The Ribbon at the top contains all menus: Home, Insert, Page Layout, Formulas, Data, Review, View.
  2. 2
    Click any cell and type to enter data. Use the Formula Bar (above the grid) to see and edit the full content of any cell.
  3. 3
    Start a formula by typing =. Common ones: =SUM(A1:A10), =AVERAGE(B2:B50), =IF(C2>100,"Yes","No").
  4. 4
    Press Ctrl + T on any data range to convert it to an Excel Table. Tables get auto-filters, banded rows, and easier formula references.
  5. 5
    For financial summaries, use PivotTables: select your data → Insert → PivotTable. Drag fields into Rows, Columns, Values to instantly summarize thousands of rows.
  6. 6
    Always save a backup before major edits to client files: File → Save As, name it with the date, e.g. ClientReport_2025-09-05_BACKUP.xlsx.

Xero
Cloud Accounting · Bank Reconciliation · Invoices · Reports
What It Is

Xero is cloud-based accounting software for small to mid-sized businesses. It handles bank feeds, invoicing, expense tracking, payroll integration, and financial reporting. Xero is hugely popular among Australian, UK, and global service business clients.

Why We Use It

Xero clients trust their books to be clean, current, and audit-ready. As an Accounts Sidekick, you'll handle daily reconciliation, invoice creation, expense entries, and report runs. Even a 24-hour delay on reconciliation creates messy books that take days to fix later.

Step-by-Step · Bank Reconciliation

Reconciliation = matching imported bank transactions to invoices, bills, or expenses inside Xero. The goal: every transaction the bank shows must have a matching record in Xero.

  1. 1
    Log in at xero.com. From the dashboard, click the bank account name (e.g. "Business Checking") to open it.
  2. 2
    You'll land on the Reconcile tab. Each row shows a bank transaction Xero needs to match.
  3. 3
    Xero often suggests a match — if correct (matching amount, date, contact), click OK. The transaction reconciles instantly.
  4. 4
    If no match exists, click Create to add a new bill or expense entry. Fill in the contact, account category, description, and amount, then click OK.
  5. 5
    For frequent recurring transactions (e.g. monthly subscriptions), use Bank Rules — Xero will auto-match them every time, saving hours.
  6. 6
    Continue until the page shows "All transactions reconciled". Repeat for every bank account daily.
Step-by-Step · Sending Invoices
  1. 1
    From the top menu, click Business → Invoices.
  2. 2
    Click + New Invoice in the top right.
  3. 3
    Fill in: Contact (start typing the client name — it autocompletes from your contacts list), Date, Due Date, Reference (your internal invoice number), and the line items (description, quantity, unit price, account category).
  4. 4
    Confirm tax rate (e.g. GST, VAT, No Tax) for each line item — this affects the totals and reporting.
  5. 5
    Click Approve. The invoice is now finalized.
  6. 6
    Click Send at the top. Xero opens an email window pre-filled with the client's email and the invoice attached as a PDF. Edit the message if needed, then click Send.
Step-by-Step · Looking Up Transaction History
  1. 1
    From the top menu, click Business → Bank accounts and select the relevant account.
  2. 2
    Click the Account Transactions tab — this shows every reconciled transaction with date, contact, description, and amount.
  3. 3
    Use the Search bar at the top to filter by contact name, amount, or reference. You can also filter by date range.
  4. 4
    To see ALL invoices and bills for a specific contact, go to Contacts → click the contact's name. Their full transaction history appears.
  5. 5
    For deeper analysis, run a General Ledger Report: Accounting → Reports → General Ledger. This shows every transaction across every account.

❓ FAQ

Day-to-Day Questions, Answered.

The questions every Sidekick asks in their first few weeks — answered upfront so you can keep moving.

1
What do I do if a client's tool login doesn't work?
+

Stop and follow the protocol — never guess passwords or repeatedly try logins, since most platforms lock the account after 3–5 failed attempts and that creates a bigger problem.

  • Check the password manager first. Look in LastPass or 1Password (whichever your client uses). Usernames and passwords for tools are stored there with the latest credentials.
  • Confirm you're using the right login URL. Some tools have multiple sub-domains (e.g. agency vs sub-account in GHL). The wrong URL with the right credentials still fails.
  • Never click "Forgot Password" on a client's tool. This sends a reset link to the owner's email, which can break automations and cause confusion.
  • Ping your Team Lead immediately in Slack/Discord. Include: the tool name, the URL you tried, and a screenshot of the error. They'll either provide updated credentials or escalate to the client.
  • While you wait, switch to other work. Don't sit idle — move to the next task on your list and circle back when access is restored.
2
How often should I update our internal task tracker?
+

Three times a day — at the start, middle, and end of every shift. Consistency here is the difference between a Sidekick people trust and one they have to chase.

  • Start of shift: Open the tracker, mark which tasks you're picking up, set their status to "In Progress." Add a quick comment if anything changed since yesterday (e.g. "Client added a new urgent priority — switching focus").
  • Middle of shift: Quick check-in. Mark anything you've completed as "Done." If you're stuck on something, change its status to "Blocked" and add a comment explaining what's blocking you and what you've tried.
  • End of shift: Update the status of everything you touched. Hand off any in-progress work clearly — note where you left off so the next person (or your future self) can pick up cleanly. Mark tasks "Complete" only when truly done, not "almost done."
  • Why this matters: Sidekick is a team-based model. Other Sidekicks, your team lead, and clients all rely on the tracker being current. A 4-hour delay can cascade into missed deadlines or duplicated work.
3
How do I communicate with clients if I am stuck on a technical issue?
+

Be transparent, present the solutions you've already tried, and ask for clarification early. Clients respect a Sidekick who shows their work — silence or vague "still working on it" replies erode trust fastest.

  • Don't go silent. If you've been stuck for more than 30 minutes, message the client (or your team lead first if the client doesn't expect direct contact). Silence breeds anxiety.
  • Use this template: "Hi [name] — I've hit a roadblock on [task]. So far, I've tried: 1) [option A], 2) [option B]. The issue I'm seeing is [specific error or behavior]. Could you confirm [specific question] or do you have a preference for how I proceed?"
  • Show your work. Include screenshots, error messages, or a quick Loom video. Don't make the client guess — give them everything they need to help you in one message.
  • Ask one specific question, not "what should I do?" Vague open-ended questions force the client to think for you. Specific questions ("Should I use approach A or B?") get faster, clearer answers.
  • Escalate to your team lead first when possible. If it's a tool issue or process question, your team lead probably knows the answer. Save the client's time for things only they can decide.
  • Never pretend you know. If the client gives you instructions that don't match what you see, ask. "I see X on my side instead of Y — can you confirm I'm in the right place?" is always better than guessing and breaking something.
